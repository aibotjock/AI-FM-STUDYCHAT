# Reference provenance audit

Reviewed 2026-10-10. Scope: the extracted reference library, not the question bank, practice engine, or a new clinical review of the app.

## Result

- 429 distinct reference URLs and 523 metadata records screened.
- 94 catalogue hostnames reviewed for publisher identity.
- No disreputable or unattributed publisher was identified in this screen; 0 references removed on publisher-reputation grounds.
- All catalogue URLs use HTTPS, have a title-bearing record, and are unique by exact URL.
- The 75-record registry and 17 project research documents remain provenance records. They are not independently approved medical guidance.

A reputable host alone does not establish the accuracy, current status, jurisdictional applicability, evidence quality, or reuse rights of an individual document. This branch is a reference directory, not a clinically validated corpus. This audit did not open and medically revalidate every article or retest every link.

## Retention criteria

Retain identifiable government/public-health agencies, professional medical societies, academic clinical institutions, specialist guideline collaborations, and established scholarly publishers or bibliographic repositories. Reject anonymous medical claims, commercial advice with no identified clinical authorship, predatory publications, fabricated citations, and documents whose claimed issuer cannot be established.

Bibliographic indexes, patient education pages, press releases, guideline landing pages, individual studies and clinical guidelines serve different purposes. Their inclusion does not make them interchangeable evidence.

## Existing qualifications preserved

Access-blocked pages and pending licenses are not publisher credibility failures. The existing item-specific restrictions remain in the registry and reuse policy; this audit grants no new copying, processing or commercial-use permission.

Metadata mentioning a retired AAP UTI guideline, a superseded NCCN PDF, or superseded ATA pregnancy guidance describes prior exclusions or version cautions. It does not make those societies' guideline directories disreputable. Those notes remain visible. No excluded obsolete document was newly imported.

Historical research notes may contain tentative conclusions, licensing assessments or the earlier RAG design. Treat them as project history, outside the medical evidence directory and normal chat context. Read the cited primary source before using a note to support a clinical answer.
