## Permission assessment and selected scope

The [CDC agency-materials policy](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html), inspected October 10, states that most CDC text is public domain, while contractor, grantee, third-party and certain other materials can be restricted. Selected documents below identify CDC as their content source and do not mark the selected agency clinical text as a third-party work. **Only those selected text passages are imported.** The app does not import the page images, agency marks, linked journal articles, university resources, external society guidelines or their tables.

CDC's reuse conditions include attribution, prominent nonendorsement, preservation of substantive content, a statement that the original is available free on the agency site, and direct links to that site. Source metadata supplies attribution/free-availability/nonendorsement statements. The host must display them prominently and open source links separately. Quotations preserve the original clinical language, exceptions and setting; only layout whitespace and PDF line-break artifacts are normalized. App titles and scope notes are separately identified editorial labels.

The [VA copyright policy](https://department.va.gov/copyright-policy/), inspected October 10, distinguishes government-produced materials from copyrighted material that VA can own by transfer. The selected recommendations here are VA/DoD guideline-work-group recommendation text in agency-issued summaries. This assessment excludes reproduced DSM diagnostic criteria, questionnaire items, licensed screening tools, externally adapted tables, journal papers, media, logos and contractor methodology text. It does **not** authorize wholesale ingestion of everything on a federal website. These are document- and passage-specific U.S. reuse assessments, not worldwide legal clearance or approval to activate a clinician-approved commercial corpus.

Every source includes the agency, exact URL, edition, source check date, locator, attribution, derivative-selection notice, U.S. applicability and limitations. Every imported section carries an October 10 source check expiring November 9, 2026, selected passage text, and its SHA-256. Some sections assemble separately located recommendations or paragraphs from the same document; their locator identifies the selected units, and they are not represented as a continuous full-page transcript. No clinical words or qualifiers are rewritten. The hash protects the selected local passage from silent change; it is **not** proof of remote-document authenticity or an independent clinical review. Previously verified material is not given a new date merely because this module was added.

## Currency and exceptions preserved

Official VA listing pages checked during this work still identify [PTSD 2023](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/mh/ptsd/), [MDD 2022](https://healthquality.va.gov/healthquality/guidelines/MH/mdd/), [SUD 2021](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/sud/), [Insomnia/OSA 2025](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/CD/insomnia/), [T2DM 2023](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/cd/diabetes/index.asp) and the new [Hypertension 2026](https://healthquality.va.gov/healthquality/guidelines/CD/htn/) editions. The provider-summary links were checked against those current listings. This establishes which agency edition was linked on the check date; it does not mean the underlying evidence search reached October 2026.

The selected PTSD summary states its evidence cutoff as May 4, 2022; SUD states June 30, 2020; hypertension 2026 states November 12, 2025. These limits remain visible in metadata or this audit. The CDC TB page explicitly links the 2025 ATS/CDC/ERS/IDSA update; this work imports CDC's own clinical-care text, not that coauthored journal document. Monthly source maintenance must inspect the selected document and its official landing/index page for updates and corrections, preserve a change history, and re-review affected content before any replacement becomes eligible.

Editorial review deliberately preserved these boundaries:

- TB's four-month option retains age, weight, drug-susceptibility, HIV CD4/ART-interaction and contraindication conditions. Nine months is conditional, not every pulmonary-TB course. Resistant, extrapulmonary, pregnancy and neonatal protocols are not supplied by this module.
- HCV requires detectable RNA and preserves pregnancy/young-child exceptions. The CDC page's sentence about cure being established “after 12 weeks of treatment” is ambiguous about timing relative to completion. It is **excluded** rather than silently converted into an unsupported SVR12 rule. Named DAA combinations, cirrhosis regimens and doses remain gaps.
- HBV's maternal DNA threshold retains CDC's explicit attribution to AASLD. HBIG is not claimed for every exposure. The page does not establish a complete chronic-HBV first-line algorithm or all newborn timing/weight exceptions.
- Influenza's uncomplicated duration, ordinary exposure prophylaxis and institutional-outbreak prophylaxis remain separate. Its renal-adjustment paragraph does not supply a numeric dosing table.
- Prazosin has a weak-for recommendation for PTSD nightmares and a weak-against recommendation for overall PTSD; these are separate indications. Trazodone's chronic-insomnia recommendation must not be confused with its MDD option.
- AUD withdrawal alternatives retain mild-to-moderate severity and benzodiazepine-risk qualifiers. They are not a substitute for the existing monitored severe-withdrawal recommendation. Clonidine/lofexidine's opioid-withdrawal role does not establish ongoing OUD relapse/overdose protection.
- VA/DoD's usual T2DM target range is **source- and population-specific**. Hypoglycemia suitability, comorbidities and life expectancy remain essential. No universal ABFM numeric target is inferred.
- The 2026 VA/DoD diastolic-goal and initial-combination-therapy statements differ from existing AHA/ACC sources. Retrieval and answer review must retain named sources and describe disagreements rather than blend them into one rule. This module does not change the existing AHA/ACC records or claim that ABFM endorses a particular organization's target.

No unsupported question is added. A generated quiz still requires a separately approved answer and exact eligible support in the host's question-release gate. Having these sources loaded is not, by itself, permission to invent a vignette, an antibiotic dose, a contraindication, a universal next step or an examination-key preference.
