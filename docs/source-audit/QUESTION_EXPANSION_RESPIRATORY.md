# Respiratory, infectious, gastrointestinal and common-condition question expansion

Authoring/source checks: **2026-10-10**. Content files: `content/conditions/respiratory_infectious.json` and `content/conditions/common_additions.json`. This contribution adds original, hypothetical five-choice board-study questions to the 30 existing conditions in these files. The frozen source/question manifest below records the complete contribution.

## Scope and preservation

- Existing condition identities, aliases, sources, source-check dates, sections, questions and answer keys are retained. Newly read sources receive separate source IDs; an October 10 check is not retroactively assigned to an older source record. File envelopes remain October 9, the earliest record check. Changed condition authoring checks are October 10; expiry remains November 9.
- New questions are application or next-step study exercises. Every new question has five choices, one canonical answer, four distractor rationales, a learning objective and exact source/section bindings. Distractors are explicitly incorrect alternatives, not approved teaching statements.
- New questions use agency-authored public-domain CDC, NIDDK, NHLBI or FDA text from the exact verified documents in the source manifest. They do not use restricted IDSA, ATS, AGA, ACG, AAAAI, HSE or other society text merely because a source is publicly readable. Old-source preservation does not confer a newly verified commercial license on old material.
- All additions remain original summaries with `reuse: original-summary-no-full-text`. No full source text, publication table, figure, photograph, logo, PDF or proprietary visual was imported. Citations link to freely available originals; agency references do not imply agency endorsement.
- Every new rights record has `commercialClinicalApproval: false`; all condition reviews remain `humanReviewed: false`. Public-domain reuse verification and clinician/commercial content approval are distinct. This is an authoring contribution for a study-only app, not a clinician-approved reference, medical advice, clinical software, official ABFM content or an ABFM pass guarantee.

## Reuse evidence and boundaries

- [CDC Use of Agency Materials](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html): most federal CDC text is public domain, with explicit exceptions for contractors, grantees, separately copyrighted/third-party material, state/local works and images. Requirements include attribution, prominent nonendorsement, substantive fidelity and notice that originals are free. No agency-wide approval is inferred for linked publications; international rights require separate consideration.
- [NIDDK copyright policy](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/copyright): most agency material is copyright free, with exceptions for third-party or sponsored works and graphics. The policy also prohibits implying endorsement or using content to recommend specific medical advice, treatments or referrals. New questions use original summaries of the exact agency-authored health-information text for hypothetical study decisions, not real-care recommendations, credited visuals or outside publications. No logos or endorsement are retained; preserve the host's actual-care boundary.
- [NHLBI trademark, branding and logo policy](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/about/contact/trademark-branding-and-logo): government text is generally public domain with marked exceptions. New questions use the specific agency clinician guide and agency health-information text only; no logos or publication layouts are reproduced.
- [FDA website policies](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/about-website/website-policies), Linking/Copying section: public-domain agency website material may be reused with acknowledgment, except material carrying a copyright notice. New allergy questions use the FDA-authored drug-safety communication text, not branding or proprietary assets.

Each source is scoped to the United States and independently recorded with its exact canonical URL, owner, policy URL, source-check date and limitations. `basis: public-domain-government` is source-specific editorial metadata; it does not mean every document on a government domain is eligible.

## Clinical and currency limits

- CDC pediatric outpatient material is used only for AOM. The page's UTI row references the retired 2011 AAP guideline and is excluded; no pediatric UTI facts are imported or taught by this contribution.
- Older NIDDK education pages are labeled with their actual review dates. Reading a page in 2026 does not turn it into a newly issued or systematically graded guideline. Drug ranks, doses, procedure details and conditional recommendations absent from the read source remain outside the new questions.
- Acute HIV testing uses CDC-authored guidance. No blanket public-domain status is claimed for coauthored NIH/HIV-panel publications or linked full guideline PDFs.
- Gonorrhea treatment failure remains suspected until investigated; persistent symptoms do not prove resistance. Severe/widespread treatment-resistant ringworm concerns similarly prompt reassessment rather than a fabricated resistance diagnosis or regimen.
- Pregnancy, age, exposure, disease site and severity modifiers are retained. Scabies oral-ivermectin safety is described as unestablished in pregnancy and children under 15 kg, rather than rewritten as established safety or an invented dosing exception.
- No new drug doses were invented. If a question concerns a drug class or pair, it explicitly does not set a patient-specific regimen. Source-limited treatment selection remains distinct from real clinical prescribing.
