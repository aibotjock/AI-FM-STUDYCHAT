# Foundations of care: educational source audit

Checked against actual primary pages on **2026-10-09**; eligible source-check period ends **2026-11-09**. This is an automated editorial source check, not clinician review, ABFM approval, CME accreditation, commercial content licensing or a guarantee of accuracy.

`content/board-foundations.json` contains six **non-disease study topics**, 18 short original teaching sections and 12 original single-best-answer application questions. These topics do **not** increase the 105-condition diagnosis count. Every vignette and numerical example is invented for exam study. The material is not medical advice, a diagnostic service or instructions for patient care.

The [current ABFM blueprint](https://www.theabfm.org/family-medicine-exam-blueprint/) was read directly. Foundations of Care has a 5% target and includes statistics, health policy, legal issues, health equity and other topics. The modules below cover selected statistics, communication, improvement and equity concepts. They do not establish exhaustive coverage of all ABFM objectives; health policy and legal topics require additional source-supported questions. The app and these questions are independent of ABFM and are not official exam items.

All sources are US federal primary educational references or recommendations. They are not mislabeled as disease-specific clinical guidelines. Only original short teaching and original questions are incorporated; no source document, copyrighted exam bank or copied vignette is stored. Federal publication does not by itself establish rights for any third-party content appearing on a page. No third-party quoted passages, illustrations or embedded examples are included.

The CDC Measures of Association page is an **archived 2012 reference** used for stable statistical definitions, not a current treatment guideline. Its worked examples were not reused. The app's original risk ratio is `(10/200)/(20/200) = 0.50`. Its original positive predictive value is `90/(90+90) = 50%`; sensitivity would instead use `90/(90+10) = 90%`. These arithmetic statements are educational, not patient data or estimated treatment effectiveness.

Publication dates are present only where displayed with a complete date. AHRQ monthly review dates remain descriptive edition metadata with `publishedDate: null`; no day of publication is invented. The CDC bias page displays August 14, 2024 at the top and August 13 in its footer; the record retains the top display date without inferring a new edition. The Field Epidemiology chapter's exact edition/date is not asserted when absent from the checked section.

New-scope validation passed once with zero rejected records: six topics, 18 sections, 12 distinct question IDs, five choices per question, one canonical keyed answer and all four distractor explanations. All section/question references resolve; all source URLs satisfy the server's official HTTPS host restrictions. Correct-answer positions are distributed A:2, B:3, C:2, D:3, E:2. Canonical question fronts remain within recall-card limits.

A deliberately conservative source budget counts every section title/text and every complete source-linked question, including invented stem, options, explanation, distractor explanations and objective. Across the 13 URLs, counts range from 26 to 179 words and remain below 200 words per URL. None of these URLs duplicates an existing condition-record source at authoring time. No quotation is incorporated. Broader integration checks should preserve this combined budget if these URLs are reused later.

No paid model call or unchanged passing suite was rerun for this authoring work. The files are ready for the separate board-session engine to load under the existing condition-shaped validator while retaining the non-disease classification.
