# Individually licensed guideline additions

Checked on 2026-10-10. This audit accompanies `content/licensed-source-additions.json`, a merge bundle rather than an independently loaded curriculum file. It adds 18 original five-choice questions: 12 US board-study candidates and six explicitly marked `examScope: comparative-study` items (two UK NOGG and four resource-constrained ASCO). Comparative items must remain available for topic study but must not inflate the US blueprint board-bank count or enter mixed US board sessions. The bundle also adds selected teaching excerpts for four existing conditions and three new disease modules. Existing content and stable question IDs are preserved. No tests, paid model calls, Git operations or deployment operations were performed by this authoring task.

These are source-checked study drafts. Every new disease module has `humanReviewed: false`; every reuse record has `commercialClinicalApproval: false`. A permissive article license permits the described reuse but does not establish clinical correctness, current US guideline completeness, ABFM endorsement or readiness for paid clinical use. No full article, figure, table, image, third-party work or dose table is copied. Summaries and fictional questions are original paraphrases with author, article, journal, DOI, license and change attribution.

The JAMA source rights objects deliberately use `cc-by-unversioned` and the exact publisher permission URL. Their article links establish CC-BY, but the exposed publisher permissions page does not name a license version or link a numbered Creative Commons license. Labeling these documents CC BY 4.0 would overstate the evidence. All six source objects retain attribution and a derivative notice outside the four-key rights object.

All questions are fictional, original and tied to specific supporting sections and the document's scope. They use distinct learning objectives from the current q1–q4 items of the four existing conditions. NOGG question stems explicitly identify their UK context, and ASCO metastatic stems identify the resource-constrained context; their comparative marker prevents importing that context into US exam coverage. Cervical q5/q6 are deliberately not filled with unsupported algorithm details. Correct options and distractor reasons describe the cited source, rather than inventing broader treatment claims.

This addition does not establish complete family medicine coverage or predict an ABFM score. It increases referenced teaching while making evidence, access, population, jurisdiction, licensing and clinical-review holes inspectable. Monthly maintenance must check current primary articles, corrections, successor guidelines and permissions without treating a refreshed byline as a new clinical evidence review.
