# Curriculum gaps: board study and broader family-medicine education

Prepared 2026-10-10. This is an editorial inventory and prioritization report, not a clinical review, an exhaustive ABFM syllabus, or a competence assessment. StudyChat is for educational study only. It must not guide diagnosis, triage, prescribing, or treatment for an actual person.

The data bank now supplies substantially more referenced study material, but it is not yet a complete family-medicine board course. The largest remaining gaps concern preventive services, undifferentiated presentations, emergency breadth, longitudinal maternity and child development, treatment depth, multimorbidity, and qualified review. More condition names or four questions per condition do not by themselves close those gaps.

The frozen [combined-bank manifest](../content/board-bank-manifest.json), checked 2026-10-10, records **117 disease records plus seven additional study topics / 546 questions**, **six foundation topics / 20 questions**, and **566 questions overall**. Strict technical validation passed for the frozen inventory. It does not certify medical accuracy, clinical review, commercial readiness, item difficulty, or curriculum completeness. Document-specific reuse assessments cover 422 questions; 344 questions have complete document-reuse annotations, and 144 question keys still have unresolved document-reuse status. A formal-source label establishes source presence, not sufficient guideline extraction, treatment depth, current U.S. applicability, or reuse permission. ## External scope context

The [official ABFM blueprint](https://www.theabfm.org/family-medicine-exam-blueprint/) organizes questions by clinical activity. Its public five-domain weights are:

| ABFM domain | Target allocation | Current U.S. pool items | Inventory concern |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Acute Care and Diagnosis | 35% | 211 | Condition-focused questions do not exhaust undifferentiated diagnosis, initial treatment, or next-step decisions. |
| Chronic Care Management | 25% | 201 | Numerous records exist, but escalation, exceptions, longitudinal follow-up, and interacting conditions need more depth. |
| Emergent and Urgent Care | 20% | 53 | Several major emergencies are represented; the range of urgent hypothetical scenarios remains limited. |
| Preventive Care | 15% | 73 | Selected immunization, reproductive planning and developmental topics now exist; comprehensive screening and well-child/preconception coverage remain incomplete. |
| Foundations of Care | 5% | 20 | Statistics, communication, quality/safety, and social needs exist; policy, legal/ethical context, and additional quantitative interpretation remain thin. |

ABFM does not publish an exhaustive condition list on this public blueprint page. This report's topic recommendations are editorial inferences from the app inventory, not an official ABFM checklist, frequency ranking, or claim about future exam questions. The manifest includes a canonical item-level crosswalk based on publisher-authored primary-domain metadata; alignment has not been independently adjudicated. Task, setting, and urgency determine the category, rather than the condition name alone. The current distribution is weighted toward chronic-care questions, with fewer urgent and preventive items relative to the blueprint. Neither question allocation nor matching the target percentages establishes complete coverage.

For broader academic scope, the [ACGME 2026 Family Medicine requirements, including FAQs](https://www.acgme.org/globalassets/pfassets/programrequirements/2026-prs/120_familymedicine_2026.pdf), effective July 1, 2026, provide context beyond disease recall: lifespan and setting breadth, maternity/newborn education, mental health, procedures, musculoskeletal/skin topics, older-adult complexity, population health, and system learning. Relevant pointers are sections 4.4, 4.5, and 4.11. ACGME material is used here only as linked scope metadata and brief original paraphrase. No protected rubric, competency rating, or requirements text is imported into the study corpus. These standards concern supervised professional education; this app does not replace that education or demonstrate clinical competence.

## Treatment, antibiotic, and next-step depth audit

The following is an audit queue, not treatment guidance or a claim that every listed element is absent. A source may contain a concept that the current app has not extracted into an eligible teaching section or question.

| Audit axis | Current evidence / example | Gap to verify before calling coverage complete |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis and uncertainty | Many questions supply an established diagnosis; others test one confirmatory method | Differential alternatives, probability, test sequencing, nondiagnostic results, discordance, and a genuinely justified next step. |
| Initial versus ongoing management | CAP has a nonsevere inpatient treatment-class question; many chronic records have selected initial choices | Outpatient/inpatient distinctions, first-line versus alternatives, escalation, reassessment, and individualized constraints. |
| Medicines and special populations | Selected pregnancy, age, comorbidity, and safety exceptions exist | Which exceptions are actually supported, which remain unavailable, and where a drug class cannot be generalized across conditions. |
| Antibiotics | STI, cystitis, plague/tularemia, and adult osteomyelitis/endocarditis have specific scoped material | Indication versus no antibiotic, microbiology, susceptibility, allergies, resistance uncertainty, route, duration, response review, and stewardship. Doses, units, renal adjustment, pregnancy, and pediatric constraints require exact source-backed objectives; Eighteen new CDC/FDA-linked items add selected chlamydia, gonorrhea, GAS-pharyngitis and epididymitis regimen/next-step detail. This does not establish complete regimens for other infections or unsupported populations; FDA approval is distinct from a CDC first-line recommendation. |
| Urgent decision tasks | Emergency-condition records plus alarm-sign questions | Educational recognition, next action, response to deterioration, and transfer/disposition reasoning within source scope; do not issue operational patient-care protocols. |
| Follow-up and longitudinal care | Examples include postpartum diabetes screening and chronic-treatment monitoring | Time horizons, recurrence, treatment failure, adverse effects, transitions, competing conditions, and prevention across lifespan. |
| Board-item depth | Disease/topic records now contain four to ten items; foundations have uneven item counts | Multiple distinct objectives, plausible alternatives, reasoning/application variety, fresh contexts, and sufficient examples across populations. Four keys do not prove mastery, validity, or syllabus completion. |
| Conversational retrieval | Responses can use only eligible source-backed spans | Compare users' unanswered study questions with missing topics, unsupported depth, stale evidence, rights exclusions, and retrieval errors; a refusal is not evidence that the medical proposition is false. |

## Source, jurisdiction, and freshness limits

An official clinical-reference page is useful for supported explanations, but it is not automatically a formal practice guideline. A record carrying a formal-guideline label may still have narrow teaching coverage, a restricted publisher, older evidence, or a population outside the intended U.S. board context. Count these as separate dimensions rather than one “covered” flag.

Document-specific government text and CC BY candidates require exact acquisition/reuse review, attribution, correction checks, and third-party exclusions. Government hosting does not clear every PDF, image, questionnaire, quoted item, or linked article. A source citation does not supply commercial RAG, AI-processing, storage, subscriber-output, or update rights. The registry and [commercial reference research](source-audit/COMMERCIAL_REFERENCE_RESEARCH.md) are a candidate/licensing map, not executed permissions. Clinical approval remains a separate unresolved gate.

Preserve explicit applicability labels. Integrated international material includes UK-specific DKA/osteoporosis guidance, Australasian bronchiolitis guidance, and international adult WikiGuidelines consensus; ASCO resource-stratified documents address particular settings and resources. The eight explicitly comparative questions are already excluded from U.S. board sampling. Other source-specific population limits still matter even when a question is in that pool. International material must not be silently substituted for U.S.-specific recommendations, screening pathways, available products, or ABFM expectations. Exact CC BY article status does not extend to every ASCO document or companion publication.

The source checks on October 9–10, 2026 document an access/content review, not a new medical publication date. Some added NIAMS pages retain 2022–2023 review dates and the NIDCD balance page retains a 2018 update; stable definitions can remain useful while treatment currency still requires scrutiny. Monthly maintenance is recorded in [STUDY_MATERIAL.md](STUDY_MATERIAL.md) and [GUIDELINE_RAG.md](GUIDELINE_RAG.md): first day of the month at 08:00 America/New_York, beginning November 1, 2026. This report preserves that schedule and claims no future execution. Maintenance must examine replacements, corrections, withdrawals, conflicts, source rights, and affected content; fetching a page successfully must not renew its clinical check automatically. Unsupported, expired, or uncertain evidence must retain the existing quarantine/withhold behavior.
